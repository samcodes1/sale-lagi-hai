Suggested fixes:
- **R1:** Add `and exists (select 1 from public.cities ci where ci.id = t.city_id and ci.is_live)` in the local branch, or reuse `private.shown_in_city(d.id, d.scope, n.city_id)`.
- **R2:** In `follows_deal`, match a city follow only when `scope = 'local'` and a target has that city. Match a category follow on `category` **or** its parent (pass the parent, or join `categories`).
- **R5:**
  - **Nearby:** compute `(deal_id, min(distance))` from `outlets_near` joined to the deal ids (deals and targets only), order and `LIMIT` that, then join `deal_cards` for the ≤ 50 winners.
  - **Trending:** rank ids from `recent_save_counts` plus `deals_live_idx` first, then join cards.
  - **Following-only:** inline the follows check (an invoker `exists` on `follows` with `user_id = auth.uid()`, which RLS allows for signed-in users; return early for anon), or pre-compute the followed brand, category and bank ids once in a CTE.
  - **Search:** cap `search_hits` (for example `limit 250` by rank inside the function).
  - Then extend `210_query_plans` to cover these four.

## Recommendations (non-blocking)
- Anon can `select` `deals` and `deal_cards` directly, with any PostgREST filter, order and offset (`max_rows` 100). The 50-row RPC pages don't slow scraping, and `ilike` filters on raw tables can't use indexes under RLS. Consider exposing only the RPCs plus `deal_cards?id=eq.` (for example a `security_invoker` view restricted by id), or rely on statement timeouts and document the choice.
- `deal_cards` has no description or terms, yet API.md says the detail page reads it. Add `description_*` and `terms_*`, or document reading `deals` for the detail page.
- An opaque cursor string (`published_at` as text with µs, plus the id) would remove R6 for every client.

## Fixes after round 1 (for re-test)
- **2.3-R1, fixed.** `nearby_deals` shows a local deal only through a target city that is live.
- **2.3-R2, fixed.** `follows_deal` now follows the alert planner's rules: brand; category or its parent; a bank on the card offers; and, for local deals only, a targeted city. The caller's follows come from `private.my_follows()` once per query, not once per row. The demo backend follows the same rules, with tests.
- **2.3-R5, fixed.**

  - `trending_deals` ranks ids first, then builds cards.
  - The following-only feed uses the inlined check above.
  - `search_hits` returns at most the best 250 hits.
- **2.3-R3 and R4 (S4), fixed along the way.** A deal is featured once, at its best placement. `category_ids = '{}'` means any.
